Our Mission & Science

About Myth Rx: Busting Health Hoaxes

Health misinformation kills. Myth Rx bridges the gap between complex peer-reviewed medical science and viral internet claims.

Why Myth Rx Was Created

Unlike political or celebrity rumors, medical misinformation carries immediate physical risk. When a patient drinks toxic kerosene or boils poisonous herbs because of a viral WhatsApp forward, or discontinues prescribed insulin or chemotherapy in favor of unverified supplements, the consequence can be fatal.

Myth Rx was developed as a dedicated health fact-checking platform. Our mission is to dismantle viral pseudoscientific advice, predatory wellness scams, and dangerous home remedies by presenting transparent, accessible, peer-reviewed clinical evidence.

Our 4-Stage Fact-Checking Methodology

Every investigation published on Myth Rx adheres to a structured, evidence-based evaluation process modeled after health journalism standards:

1. Claim Selection & Harm Assessment

We prioritize claims based on three metrics: virality (spread across messaging apps and social media), potential for acute harm (claims encouraging patients to abandon critical medications, ingest toxic substances, or delay emergency care), and predatory exploitation (financial wellness and miracle cure schemes).

2. Hierarchy of Medical Evidence

We do not rely on anecdotal testimonials, press releases, or commercial wellness blogs. We evaluate claims against the established hierarchy of scientific evidence: systematic reviews and meta-analyses (Cochrane, PubMed), randomized controlled trials (RCTs), national clinical practice guidelines (e.g. AUA, ADA, AHA), and global public health surveillance (WHO, CDC, FDA).

3. Standardized Rating Verdicts

Every investigated claim receives one of four standardized public verdicts:

  • SUPPORTED: Clinical trials, systematic reviews, and professional consensus guidelines corroborate the claim's health statements. (Note: We use "Supported" rather than "Verified" because verification describes our investigatory workflow, not truth itself).
  • FALSE: The claim is demonstrably inaccurate, contradicted by clinical trials, or physiologically impossible.
  • MISLEADING: Contains an isolated laboratory truth or preliminary animal observation that has been exaggerated, stripped of crucial context, or generalized to false human health advice.
  • UNVERIFIED: Sufficient human clinical evidence does not exist to either prove or disprove the claim. Preliminary in-vitro studies cannot justify clinical practice.

4. Patient-First Plain-Language Translation

Medical evidence is only protective if ordinary families can understand and share it. We translate dense pharmacological pathways into clear, accessible explanations that readers can send directly to relatives to counter viral misinformation in messaging groups.

Editorial Transparency & Review Integrity

Myth Rx is developed as an academic fact-checking initiative. We adhere to strict integrity and transparency principles:

  • Honest Attribution: We describe our methodology transparently. We never claim a specialist or medical expert has reviewed an article unless that clinical review has formally taken place and is documented.
  • Zero Commercial Conflicts: We accept zero pharmaceutical sponsorships, supplement affiliate commissions, or sponsored medical placements.
  • Direct Citations: Every factual claim in our evaluations links directly to indexed, peer-reviewed medical publications or public health authority repositories.
  • Corrections Policy: If new peer-reviewed clinical evidence emerges, our investigations are updated with a transparent correction log.

Platform Scope & Limitations

Understanding what Myth Rx is—and what it is not—is critical to using this platform responsibly:

  • General Education Only: Investigations assess general population-level health claims. They cannot account for individual comorbidities, drug interactions, or personal clinical histories.
  • Not an Emergency Service: We do not triage acute symptoms or provide real-time clinical consultations. In an emergency, always call local emergency medical services immediately.
  • Evidence-Based Scope: Published articles synthesize consensus findings from peer-reviewed medical literature and clinical guidelines.
